# Google Search's core updates and your website

> Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates
> Last updated: 2025-12-10 UTC

Several times a year, Google makes significant, broad changes to our search algorithms and systems. We refer to these as core updates, and we give notice when they happen on our list of Google Search ranking updates.

## How core updates work

Core updates are designed to ensure that overall, we're delivering on our mission to present helpful and reliable results for searchers. These changes are broad in nature, and don't target specific sites or individual web pages. As content on the web changes, we assess and update our systems to keep pace, as a whole.

One way to think about a core update is to imagine that a friend asked you for your top food recommendations. While you do have a list of your 20 favorite restaurants, things have changed since you initially wrote it. Some new restaurants that didn't exist before are now candidates for your list. You might reassess some restaurants and realize they move to a higher spot on the list. The list will change, and restaurants that move down aren't necessarily "bad"; there are just other restaurants that make your top 20.

## Check if there's a traffic drop in Search Console

1. **Confirm that the core update has finished rolling out.** Check the Search Status Dashboard and take note of the start and end date of the core update.
2. **Compare the right dates**: We recommend waiting at least a full week after a core update completes before analyzing your site in Search Console. After a week, try comparing this week with a week before the core update started rolling out.
3. **Review your top pages and queries.** Assess how they were ranking before and after the core update:
    - **Small drop in position (dropping from position 2 to 4)**: There's no need to take drastic action.
    - **Large drop in position (dropping from position 4 to 29)**: Do a deeper assessment.
4. **Analyze different search types separately**: This can help you understand whether the drop happened in Web Search, Google Images, Video mode, or the News tab.

## Assessing a large drop in position

If you're seeing a sustained, large drop in position for your site as a whole, read through the self-assessment to check if your site overall is delivering content that's helpful, reliable, and people first. Specifically:

- **Take a close look at your site as a whole, and try to be objective.** You might also ask others you trust to do an assessment.
- **Evaluate the pages that were most impacted.** Look closely at these to understand how these pages may perform against the self-assessment questions.

## Things to keep in mind when making changes

- **Avoid doing "quick fix" changes.** Instead, focus on making changes that make sense for your users and are sustainable in the long term.
- **Consider how you can improve your content in meaningful ways.** For example, it could be that rewriting or restructuring your content makes it easier for your audience to read and navigate.
- **Deleting content is a last resort,** and only to be considered if you think the content can't be salvaged.

## How long does it take to see an effect in Search results

If you've made improvements, it may take time to see an effect in Search results: some changes can take effect in a few days, but it could take several months for our systems to learn and confirm that the site as a whole is now producing helpful, reliable, people-first content in the long term. If it's been a few months and you still haven't seen any effect, that could mean waiting until the next core update.

However, you don't necessarily have to wait for a major core update to see the effect of your improvements. We're continually making updates to our search algorithms, including smaller core updates. These updates are not announced because they aren't widely noticeable, but they are another way that your content can see a rise in position.

Keep in mind that there's no guarantee that changes you make to your website will result in noticeable impact in search results, and that positions in Google Search results aren't static or fixed in place.
